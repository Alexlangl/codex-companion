## Design Context

### Users

Codex Companion is for technical power users who manage multiple Codex accounts,
provider credentials, routing groups, and a local API relay from a desktop app.
They use it repeatedly during development and need to understand account health,
the active route, and the consequence of a launch or repair action at a glance.

### Brand Personality

Calm, precise, and native. The interface should feel like a dependable desktop
utility with deliberate hierarchy, not a generic admin template, marketing
dashboard, or themed control room. It should reward expertise without becoming
cryptic or visually forceful.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a quiet native-desktop aesthetic with compact but breathable layouts and
purposeful status color. Support both light and dark themes. Draw from CC-Switch's
clear primary actions and provider identity, and Cockpit Tools' visible operational
status, while preserving Codex Companion's own logo and simpler structure.

Avoid dark sidebars in light mode, decorative grid or rule patterns, replacement
brand marks, oversized black feature blocks, saturated orange calls to action,
decorative glow, gradients, and repeated floating cards.

The current interface is the anti-reference: system-default typography, uniformly
gray surfaces, repeated bordered panels, and nearly equal visual weight across
headings, labels, status, and actions make it feel machine-generated and anonymous.

### Design Principles

1. Make the active account, route, health, and next action legible within five seconds.
2. Use spacing and alignment for structure; reserve visible rules for real boundaries
   and cards for accounts, repeated entities, and bounded tools.
3. Give providers and operational states controlled identity through icons and rare
   accent color, never through decorative gradients or glow.
4. Keep expert workflows dense and fast, with secondary actions progressively disclosed.
5. Build a coherent desktop product across both themes, including focus, loading,
   empty, error, and destructive states.
